# Retatrutide: Key Information

What it is: Retatrutide (LY-3437943) is an experimental obesity drug developed by Eli Lilly that functions as a triple glucagon hormone receptor agonist (GLP-1, GIP, and GCGR receptors).

How it works: The drug targets three receptor types. GLP-1 receptors enhance insulin sensitivity and increase satiety, while glucagon receptor activation boosts energy expenditure and fat loss.

Clinical results: According to systematic reviews, retatrutide produces substantial reductions in body weight in adults with obesity, with mean percentage weight loss typically between 15 and 24 percent over 48 to 72 weeks.

Side effects: The most common adverse effects are gastrointestinal—nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and abdominal discomfort. These increase with higher doses. Long-term safety profiles are still being evaluated.

Trial status: The drug has completed Phase 2 testing and is currently undergoing Phase 3 clinical trials, including the TRIUMPH-3 study for participants with obesity and cardiovascular disease.

Research highlights: Recent preclinical studies suggest retatrutide may help suppress tumor growth in obesity-associated cancers more effectively than competitor semaglutide.
